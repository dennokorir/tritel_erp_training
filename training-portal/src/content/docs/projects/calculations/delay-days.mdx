A client delay records a period when the client held the work up. Approved delays move the deadline that the on-time result is measured against.

## The rule

- **Days** on one delay = **To** − **From** + 1. Both the first and the last day count, so 1 to 10 March is 10 days.
- **Approved client delay (days)** on the project = the number of different calendar days covered by its **Approved** delays. A day covered by two approved delays counts once.
- Delays that are **Draft**, **Waiting for approval** or **Rejected** don't count.
- At close, the approved days are added to the approved completion date to give the deadline. See [Close figures and the Delivery KPI](/projects/calculations/close-and-kpi/).

<Illustration name="projects/calc-delay-days" caption="Two approved delays overlap on 5 to 10 March. The project has 12 approved days, not 18." />

## Edge cases

- **Calendar days.** Weekends and public holidays count.
- **Any dates.** A delay counts wherever its dates fall, before the project start or after the completion date. Check the dates before approving.
- **Same start and end.** A one-day delay (From and To the same day) is 1 day.
- **End before start** is refused: *The delay ends before it starts.*
- **Frozen at close.** Closing records the approved days as **Approved client delay at close (days)**. Delays can't be logged, approved or rejected on a closed project: *Customer A network upgrade is closed. Finance can reopen it first.*

:::note[Good to know]
The total under the **Days** column of the client delays list adds every delay in the list, overlaps and rejected delays included. The figure that counts is **Approved client delay (days)** on the project's **Handover & close** tab.

A rejected delay stays rejected: it can't go back to draft. The Head of Delivery can also reject a delay that was already approved, which takes its days off again.
:::
