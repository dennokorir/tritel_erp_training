An **OEM** (original equipment manufacturer) is a maker whose products Tritel resells, for example a network or software vendor. Tritel rarely buys from the OEM directly. It buys through a **distributor**, a supplier that carries one or more OEMs. The OEM then credits Tritel on its partner portal with its own figure for those purchases. That figure is what an [OEM booking](/tenders/channel/oem-bookings/) records.

Each OEM and each distributor has a status: **Onboarding** while the relationship is being set up, **Active** once it is running, and **Archived** when it is no longer used.

<Illustration name="tenders/ch-oem-form" caption="An OEM. The numbers match the list and steps below." />

1. Open **Channel › OEMs(Vendors)**. The list shows archived OEMs too.
2. The OEM's name.
3. **Confirm** makes an Onboarding OEM **Active** (you are asked *Set Active?*). **Draft** takes an Active OEM back to **Onboarding**.
4. The tabs: **Distributors** (who sells it to Tritel), **Solution Engineers** and **Certifications** (its courses).
5. The smart buttons: **Opportunities** (the Vendor Pipeline: opportunities with this OEM), **Purchase Orders**, **Bookings** and **Awaiting OEM** (tagged purchase lines this year with no confirmed booking; shown only when there are some).
6. **Channel performance this year (USD)**: **OEM-Recognised YTD (USD)** and **Purchase Cost YTD (USD)** from this year's confirmed bookings, the **Average Distributor Margin (%)** between them, and **Awaiting OEM Confirmation (USD)**.

## Key fields

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Account Manager** | The Tritel employee who looks after this OEM. **Phone**, **Mobile** and **Email** come from their employee record. |
| **Partner Portal** | The address of the OEM's partner portal. The OEM card in the kanban view has a button to open it. |
| **Distributors** (OEM tab) / **OEMs(Vendors)** (distributor tab) | Which distributors carry the OEM. The same link shows on both forms. |
| **Vendor** (on a distributor) | The distributor's supplier contact, the one used on purchase orders. |

## Add or update an OEM

1. Open **Channel › OEMs(Vendors)** and click **New**, or click an OEM.
2. Type the name, choose the **Account Manager** and fill in **Partner Portal**.
3. On the **Distributors** tab, add the distributors that sell this OEM to Tritel.
4. When the OEM is ready to use, click **Confirm**, then **OK** on *Set Active?*.

## Add or update a distributor

1. Open **Channel › Distributors** and click **New**, or click a distributor.
2. Type the name and choose the **Vendor**: the supplier contact used on purchase orders.
3. On the **OEMs(Vendors)** tab, add the OEMs it carries.
4. Click **Confirm**, then **OK** on *Set Active?*.

## Why the links matter

When someone adds a line to a purchase order, the system sets the line's **OEM** by itself when it can be sure:

- the product has an **OEM** (on the product's **Channel** tab); otherwise
- the supplier on the order is the **Vendor** of a distributor that carries exactly **one** OEM.

If neither applies, the line stays without an OEM and appears in [Untagged Purchases](/tenders/channel/tag-untagged-purchases/). A distributor with the right **Vendor** and a product with the right OEM save that clean-up later.

:::note[Good to know]
- Archiving an OEM or a distributor sets it to **Archived**. Unarchiving it sets it back to **Onboarding**, even if it was **Active** before. Click **Confirm** again.
- The **Purchase Orders** count on the OEM also counts cancelled purchase orders. Use **Bookings** and the channel figures for money: they leave cancelled orders out.
- Staff with CRM access can create and edit OEMs; only sales managers who see all documents can delete one. Every internal user can create, edit and delete distributors.
:::
